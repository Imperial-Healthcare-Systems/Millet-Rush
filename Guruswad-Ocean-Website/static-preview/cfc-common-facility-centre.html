Common Facility Centre

Shared infrastructure, not a private factory.

A common processing facility planned for farmers, producer companies, women’s self-help groups and small food entrepreneurs — so that a small producer can reach a market without building a plant first.

Planned project

One facility, used by everyone who needs it.

Most small millet producers lose their margin in the same place. They can grow the grain, but they cannot clean, grade, mill or pack it to the standard a buyer expects. Building a line of your own for a few tonnes a season makes no sense. Sharing one does.

The Common Facility Centre is planned as that shared line — held through Guruswad Agriculture and Food Association, and open to producers beyond our own network. The same equipment that makes a MilletRush product is intended to make someone else’s.

This is a planned project. Nothing on this page describes a facility in operation, and none of the services listed is being offered yet. The page will be updated service by service as each line is commissioned, so that what it claims and what exists stay the same thing.

Planned site of the Common Facility Centre, Maharashtra
Who it is for

Four kinds of producer, one shared line.

The facility is not being built for one company’s production run. It is being built as infrastructure — and infrastructure is judged by who else can use it.

Farmers

Members of the producer network, and growers outside it who need cleaning or milling done properly rather than roughly.

Farmer Producer Organisations

Other FPOs with produce to aggregate and no processing line of their own.

Women’s self-help groups

Groups taking on cleaning, grading, value addition and packing as paid work.

Small entrepreneurs

Small-scale food processors who need plant capacity, not a plant — and a product they can sell under their own name.

Services planned

From a sack of raw grain to a sealed, tested pack.

Six services, each one a step a small producer currently has to skip, outsource at a loss, or do badly.

Grain cleaning and grading

De-stoning, cleaning and grading by size and quality — the step that decides whether a lot can be sold at all.

Millet processing

De-hulling and milling for sorghum, pearl millet and the other millets the network grows.

Jowar puffs and other value-added products

Puffing and the value-added lines that turn a grain worth a commodity price into a product worth a shelf price.

Flour, rava and instant mixes

Milling and blending into flour, rava and instant mixes, in pack formats a small producer can actually sell.

Packaging and quality testing

Packing to retail and bulk formats, with testing against the standard the buyer is going to ask about.

Product development and processing training

Developing new formats, and training the people who will make them — so the skill stays in the village after the batch has left it.

Equipment specifications, installed capacity, tariffs and the commissioning timetable are to be confirmed by the client before publication. The six services above are the scope as the client set it out; the numbers behind them are not yet ours to publish.

Why shared

Processing is where a farmer’s margin is usually lost.

A grower who sells raw grain takes the commodity price. A grower who sells cleaned, graded, milled and packed grain takes considerably more. The difference is not the crop — it is access to a machine.

  • Capacity a small producer could not justify alone
  • One quality standard, whoever the batch belongs to
  • Training that stays with the people, not the plant
  • Women’s self-help groups paid for processing work, not only for labour
  • A route to market for producers who are not our members
